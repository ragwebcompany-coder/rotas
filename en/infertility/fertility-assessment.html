What is a fertility assessment?

During a fertility assessment, various specific tests are carried out in order to find the cause of the infertility. If a specific cause is found, there is likely to be a corresponding treatment. Often infertility can be treated successfully even when no cause is found.

When should I consider having a fertility assessment?

You should consider having a fertility assessment if any of the following applies to you:

  • You have not become pregnant after 1 year of regular intercourse without contraception.
  • You are over 35 and have not become pregnant after 6 months of regular intercourse without contraception.
  • Your menstrual cycle is not regular.
  • You or your partner have a known fertility problem.

What causes infertility?

Infertility can be caused by various factors. Both male and female factors can contribute. Female factors may include problems with ovulation, with the reproductive organs or with hormones. Male factors often involve problems with the quantity or the quality of the sperm.

Does age affect fertility?

Yes. A woman begins life with a fixed number of eggs. This number falls as she gets older. For healthy young couples, the chance of a woman becoming pregnant is about 20% in each menstrual cycle. That rate begins to fall in a woman's early thirties, and falls faster after the age of 37. A man's fertility also declines with age, but less predictably.

Can lifestyle affect fertility?

Lifestyle factors can play a part in infertility. For women, being underweight, being overweight or exercising excessively can be associated with infertility. Moderate or heavy alcohol consumption and smoking can make it harder for a woman to become pregnant. For men, smoking, heavy drinking, using marijuana and using anabolic steroids can reduce sperm count and motility. Smoking can also lead to erectile dysfunction.

How long does a fertility assessment take?

In most cases a fertility assessment can be completed within a few menstrual cycles.

What should I expect at my first fertility appointment?

The first appointment usually involves a detailed medical history and a physical examination. During the history you will be asked about your periods, any abnormal vaginal bleeding or discharge, pelvic pain and disorders that can affect reproduction, such as thyroid disease. If you have a male partner, you will both be asked about the following health matters:

  • Medicines (both prescribed and over the counter) and herbal remedies
  • Illnesses, including sexually transmitted infections, and past operations
  • Birth defects in your family
  • Previous pregnancies and their outcomes
  • Use of tobacco, alcohol and illegal drugs
  • Occupation

You and your partner will also be asked about your sexual history:

• Methods of contraception

• How long you have been trying to become pregnant

• How often you have intercourse and whether or not you have any difficulties

• Whether you use lubricants during intercourse

• Previous sexual relationships

What tests are done for infertility?

Tests for infertility include laboratory tests, imaging tests and certain procedures.

What does the basic assessment for a woman involve?

The laboratory tests include basal body temperature charting, a urine test, a progesterone test, thyroid function tests, a prolactin level and ovarian reserve tests. The imaging tests and procedures include ultrasound, hysterosalpingography, sonohysterography, hysteroscopy and laparoscopy. You may not have all of these tests and procedures. Some are done on the basis of the results of earlier tests and procedures.

What is the purpose of basal body temperature charting?

Charting the basal body temperature is a way of telling whether ovulation has occurred. After a woman ovulates, her body temperature rises slightly.

How do I chart my basal body temperature?

For this test you take your temperature by mouth every morning before getting out of bed and record it on a chart for two or three menstrual cycles.

What do the results of a urine test show?

The results of urine tests show when and whether you are ovulating. The test detects luteinizing hormone (LH) in the urine. This hormone triggers the release of an egg.

How is the progesterone test done?

A blood sample is taken on a particular day of the menstrual cycle and the level of the hormone progesterone is measured. A raised level shows that you are ovulating.

When are thyroid function tests done?

Thyroid problems can affect fertility. If a problem with your thyroid gland is suspected, the levels of the hormones that control the thyroid are measured to establish whether it is working normally.

What is the prolactin test?

This blood test measures the level of the hormone prolactin. High prolactin levels can disrupt ovulation.

What are ovarian reserve tests?

These tests measure the levels of certain hormones in the blood that are involved in ovulation. They may be recommended if you are over 35 or if you have known fertility problems. The results of these tests can give an idea of how many eggs the ovaries have and whether they are still healthy.

Why are imaging tests and procedures done?

Different imaging tests and procedures are used to examine your reproductive organs. These tests check whether your fallopian tubes are healthy and whether there are any problems in your uterus. Which procedures are used depends on your symptoms and on the results of other tests and procedures.

What does the basic assessment for a man involve?

The assessment for a man often includes a semen analysis (sperm count). If the semen analysis result is abnormal, or if there are concerning features in the man's history, other tests may be carried out. An ultrasound may be done, for example, to look for problems in the ducts and tubes through which the sperm travels. Ultrasound can also be used to find problems in the scrotum that may be causing infertility.
